Executive decisions — locked baseline
•  One authoritative Real Estate Payment Service and ledger serves every role and application; no independent
per-app ledger.
•  Every registered public user receives an internal wallet identity automatically; external virtual accounts
require consent, eligibility and provider approval.
•  Funds are collected, safeguarded and moved by approved regulated third parties. CosmicForge owns
orchestrated payment logic, records, allocations, authorization and policies.
•  Paystack is the preferred first integration, but the platform is provider-neutral at its domain boundary.
Collection, custody, wallet funding, refunds and payout rails may differ.
•  Paystack Manual Payouts is the preferred settlement arrangement where approved. Merchant balance alone
is not proof of permissible buyer-seller escrow or user stored value.
•  Beneficiary splits are computed and version-locked inside the CosmicForge ledger; unreleased shares are
visible as pending/protected and never withdrawable.
•  Withdrawal eligibility follows verifiable contractual milestones, dispute/lien holds, beneficiary validation,
settled backing and wallet limits.
•  All receiving user roles can withdraw eligible available earnings through an approved payout provider;
funding and customer withdrawal are separately permissioned.
•  Initial rental charges are separated from rent. Subsequent rent payments do not automatically repeat one-off
platform/onboarding/legal fees.
•  Recurring platform revenue defaults to a disclosed owner-side commission on rent collections (rate NOT
fixed), or a separately approved owner subscription.
•  Continuing/periodic tenancy and fixed-term agreement journeys are both supported; payment of the next
rent installment does not automatically require a new contract.
•  Customer wallet top-up and authorized spend are core; saved-bank-account auto-debit, card mandates and
automated recurring rent scheduling are deferred to later phases.
•  No go-live until approved legal/regulatory funds-handling structure, Paystack/provider commercial
agreement, and financial certification are evidenced.

1.3 Non-negotiable boundaries
•  No app or human actor may directly update spendable money; only ledger-posted, policy-authorized
operations change financial balances.
•  A business event must be authenticated, source-bound, versioned, evidence-linked, and idempotent before
affecting protected allocations.
•  Payout beneficiary must be verified against legal owner or separately documented entitlement, not the listing
creator or manager.
•  No provider-scoped balances are assumed fungible across merchants, providers, legal entities, currencies or
regulated custodial accounts.
•  No claims that funds "never leave Paystack": in manual payout arrangements funds reside in a Paystack-
identified commercial/banking structure, subject to settlement timing, terms and restrictions.
•  Production transfer and fund-holding actions default to disabled pending commercial, regulatory and tested
operational approval.

2.2 Accounting controls
•  Integer minor units and ISO currency code; currency-specific ledgers; no floating point in financial
persistence.
•  Immutable balanced journals; compensating transactions for corrections; immutable authorization/policy
snapshot for every posting.
•  A unified financial identity with separate legal-party, user-role, property/project, funding-provider,
beneficiary and transaction accounts.
•  Liability-ledger segregation for customer available balances, merchant settlement, protected funds, payout
pending, refundable deposits, provider payable and platform revenue.
•  Funds coverage = reconciled third-party cash/settled receivable by custody pool minus explicit operational
reserves, compared to customer and beneficiary liabilities. No unfunded release.

3.2 Preferred Paystack configuration
DESIGN — ENGINEERING: Use Paystack checkout/cards/bank transfer and eligible Paystack-Titan DVAs for
collections. Seek Paystack Manual Payouts for corporate merchant balances and Paystack Transfers for
beneficiary payouts. Approval and risk acceptance must be obtained first. The preferred flow is Customer  →
Paystack collection  reconciled Paystack settlement/balance  ledger pending/protected allocation  → → →
authorized release  payable/available  Paystack transfer to verified bank.→ →
GATED — EXTERNAL: Manual Payouts govern merchant settlements; they are not equivalent to licensed third-
party escrow. Paystack eligibility guidance lists escrow services as unsupported under standard terms. High-
value sales, deposits and long-duration custody require documented banking/legal structure, approved provider
product and contractual restrictions. Do not launch merely because an endpoint succeeds in test mode.
3.3 Provider routing policy and migration
•  Route based on transaction category, geography/currency, verification, legal custody capability, amount,
uptime, pricing, liquidity pool, fees and tenant/provider constraints.
•  Select and persist route before charging; never auto-retry through another provider while a prior charge or
transfer outcome is unknown.
•  For a transaction with collected funds at Provider A, payout from Provider B only after explicit approved
inter-provider funding, balance reconciliation and migration ledger journal.
•  A provider switch affects new transactions by default; in-flight transactions remain bound to original
provider/custody pool unless a controlled migration is approved.
•  Provider credentials, webhooks, modes and capabilities are per legal entity/environment, securely stored and
rotated. Country/currency support is declarative.

4. Payment lifecycle, beneficiary allocations and disbursements
4.1 Canonical transaction sequence
1. Business service locks a full disclosed quote and agreement/policy version; finance confirms permitted
jurisdiction/transaction type.
2. Core creates immutable payment intent and chosen provider route; identifies each charge component and its
payer.
3. Paystack/PSP collects; Core confirms signature/event + verified amount/currency/reference/recipient and
records clearing versus settled funding.
4. Split engine validates beneficiary authority and computes a versioned allocation snapshot with
fee/processing/tax treatment.
5. After financial confirmation, allocations appear pending/protected; no early internal spend or external payout.
6. Owning business service emits validated milestone completion evidence; release engine checks policy,
disputes, account ownership and actual funding.
7. Release eligible portions to beneficiary available balances; keep security deposits, future milestones, reserves
and disputed amounts separately.
8. Withdrawal locks available funds, uses verified destination, authenticates step-up and risk/approval, then
triggers external transfer.
9. Webhook and read-back settle terminal outcome; ledger reservations are released/consumed only on
confirmed result; notify all affected parties.
4.2 Allocation invariants
•  Gross customer payable = sum(disclosed line items) and must equal accepted quote and collected amount,
within explicitly defined permitted adjustments.
•  Sum of all beneficiary shares, authorized retained taxes/fees, protected deposits and reconciled processing
allocations = payable, with no unexplained residual.
•  All beneficiary shares are frozen to the accepted version; changing recipient or percentages requires new
approvals and a traceable correction.
•  A beneficiary share has independent hold/release/dispute state; one disputed share must not automatically
block clean portions if contract/law allows release.
•  Co-owner percentages, managers and corporate revenue shares come only from verified ownership/legal
mandates and approved beneficiary records.
•  No auto-release purely on elapsed time where evidence or legal obligations remain outstanding; timeout
actions require a policy and, where needed, human review.

6.3 Rent cycle vs contract continuity
•  Fixed-term tenancy and continuing/periodic tenancy must both be supported. A rent installment is not the
same as execution of a fresh agreement or renewal of a fixed term.
•  A continuing agreement may govern recurring rent until changed or terminated under applicable law; retain
versioned original terms, permitted rent reviews, notice periods and obligations.
•  Where law/contract requires fresh agreement or addendum for a material change, use reviewed templates
and approvals. No automatic legal charge without service and consent.
•  Payment schedule may be annual, semiannual, quarterly, monthly or another lawful agreed interval; the
base rent obligation has an immutable reference and due date.
•  Recurring wallet auto-pay requires opt-in and revocable mandate, but execution of scheduled payment
instructions and saved-bank-account charging remain DEFERRED.

7. Refundable deposits, reserves, disputes and cancellations
7.1 Deposit segregation
Security deposits, hospitality damage deposits and restoration deposits have distinct ledger liabilities, payout
rules and custody references. They cannot be treated as rent, platform revenue or owner available earnings
merely when received. Record ingress, inspection/condition evidence, deduction claim and tenant challenge
separately.
7.2 Dispute workflow
•  Dispute intake binds payment, beneficiary shares, property/job/booking, authority, documentary evidence,
deadline and review officer.
•  Freeze only contested amounts where feasible; allow uncontested portions to progress under approved
policy.
•  Decisions require separated case-worker and payout authority where thresholds dictate; every override logs
reason, signer and evidence.
•  Support outcome: release to entitled party, return to payer, partial award, reserve extension, rework or legal
escalation.
•  Cases and property records remain linked after account closure for lawful retention; explicit statutory
deposit refund deadlines and escalations must be configured by jurisdiction.
7.3 Refund, chargeback and reversal correctness
•  Track every refund attempt independently, allow multiple partial refunds with cumulative caps and race
protection; do not prematurely mark original payment refunded.
•  Reversed/charged-back charges must correctly adjust external funding, liabilities, recoverable amounts and
reserves without deleting financial history.
•  Refund provider results of timeout/unknown outcome remain reserved pending read-back; never issue blind
duplicate refund or a balance refund at the same time.
•  Refund destination must obey instrument and legal/provider rules; wallet refund is not always
interchangeable with original card/bank reversal.

8. Provider payouts and safe available balances
8.1 Verified beneficiary and mandate
•  Individual owners: beneficiary must match legal authority or documented verified payout authority; co-
owner shares follow signed entitlement and consent.
•  Manager payouts derive only from an active, property-specific management mandate and fee schedule;
managers cannot redirect owner disbursements.
•  Business payouts follow corporate identity, branch/project mapping, approved bank destination, financial
approval chain and limits.
•  Artisan and professional payouts go to the verified individual/firm named on completed work or signed
appointment, not to arbitrary staff.
•  Change of payout bank requires step-up, identity revalidation, cooling-off and notification; high-value actions
can require dual approval.
8.2 Payout state machine
REQUESTED  RISK_REVIEW / MANUAL_APPROVAL (if configured)  RESERVED  SUBMITTED  PROCESSING /→ → → →
OTP_REQUIRED / UNKNOWN_OUTCOME  CONFIRMED_PAID or CONFIRMED_FAILED / REVERSED. No terminal →
outcome without verified external evidence; never restore funds on a mere timeout. All retries preserve provider
transfer reference and idempotency.
8.3 Liquidity safety rule
AvailableBalanceDisplayed can be positive only when the policy-authorized amount is recorded as earned and
backed by reconciled custodial or settlement funds (or an explicitly authorized prefunding arrangement). Actual
withdrawal may wait for settlement/rail availability; show separate status instead of false immediate payout.
9. Customer funding and future recurring rent automation
9.1 Core customer top-up and spend
•  Customer can initiate an approved wallet top-up via PSP checkout or assigned DVA; wallet credit occurs only
after verified amount/reference/customer attribution.
•  Wallet payment of accepted rent/booking/service obligation is atomic and idempotent; debit customer
available and credit transaction protected liability.
•  Available customer funds are not automatically treated as earning for owners; settlement and business
release conditions still apply.
•  Customer wallet withdrawal/refund capabilities are separate and must not be shown as operational until
provider/compliance approval and backend certification.
9.2 Recurring payment design reservation — DEFERRED
Future work will support an opt-in autopay mandate on available wallet balance, low-balance notifications,
installment scheduling, payment retries and escalation. A stored bank account must NEVER be charged without
an authorized mandate and compliant debit rail. Saved-card authorization and direct-debit provider integrations
are explicitly out of scope for core v1 and must be separately reviewed. Do not implement fake or client-side
simulated success.

FUNDED_PROTECTED / FAILED / CANCELLED / UNKNOWN_OUTCOME. Partial capture/partial funding requires
explicit schema and policies. Provider webhooks alone are inputs, not indisputable authorization.
10.2 Beneficiary allocation lifecycle
PROPOSED  LOCKED  PENDING_FUNDING  FUNDED_HELD  PARTIALLY_RELEASED / AVAILABLE  → → → → →
WITHDRAWAL_RESERVED  PAID; alternative DISPUTED / REFUND_RESERVED / CANCELLED states. Allocation →
cannot become AVAILABLE without policy + sufficient backing + verified beneficiary.
10.3 Durability and operational safeguards
•  Transactional outbox/inbox, signature validation, event deduplication without suppressing retry of failed
events, bounded backoff and dead-letter queues.
•  Unknown external outcomes recover by read-back, not by cancellation; idempotency keys persist original
request fingerprint and final outcome.
•  Row locks/advisory locks for balances/transactions; at-most-once economic effects despite at-least-once
delivery; no phantom success.
•  Double-entry integrity checked per currency/legal entity; payout/refund transfer references unique and
reconciled.
•  Backups, point-in-time recovery, secrets rotation, service-to-service authorization, audit retention, alerting
and incident response.
•  Daily automatic reconciliation plus intraday checks when needed; provider balance, pending settlements,
fees, chargebacks, reserves, liabilities and unmatched credits.

11.1 Minimum invariant set
1. Every posted journal balances separately by currency and legal entity.
2. No ledger debit can make a restricted available balance negative absent an explicitly approved credit facility.
3. No amount may simultaneously be both protected and freely available to the same beneficiary.
4. Per-transaction beneficiary allocations reconcile exactly to verified line items and approved adjustments.
5. Cumulative captures, refunds, releases and payouts never exceed their respective funded or entitled amounts.
6. Provider funds never silently bridge legal entities, currencies, custody pools or in-flight transfers.
7. An approved bank-change cooldown or dispute blocks relevant withdrawals.
8. Cash/settlement and customer liability coverage exceptions immediately produce holds and operator alerts.

13.2 Conditions that must not be implied
•  Paystack merchant balance is not a trust account simply because it can be manually disbursed.
•  An in-app "wallet" does not by itself authorize CosmicForge to issue redeemable stored-value, hold customer
deposits or operate money transmission.
•  A tenant prepaid annual rent is not automatically lawful to hold for an arbitrary period; follow actual
contract and local law.
•  The ledger can reserve pending amounts even before custody is live, but must not label them "safeguarded"
or "escrow protected" unless the corresponding custody arrangement exists.
•  No shared Paystack balance funding model may use one customer/transaction's restricted funds to pay a
different party.

This document locks the user-approved product architecture for a centralized, provider-neutral CosmicForge
Real Estate Payment Service. It explicitly includes every public user wallet, internal beneficiary allocations,
transparent multi-party splits, milestone-based release, deposit separation, verified withdrawals, tenant top-ups,
distinct initial and recurring rent costs, continuing tenancy support and deferred saved-bank auto-debit.

the engineering agent must audit the target repository and write a source-to-contract coverage plan. Before live
money, the business must obtain provider acceptance, lawful custody/stored-value permissions, state-specific
policy approval, verified settlement arrangements and real financial E2E certification.
